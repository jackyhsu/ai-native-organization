# AI Native Organization

English | [中文](README.md)

**A live experiment in rebuilding a company around AI.**

> Can 5 humans build what used to require 50?

## The experiment in 30 seconds

Ingora is a five-person startup trying to operate across a capability surface that might once have required dozens of people: research, product, software, testing, operations, finance, compliance, and more.

AI agents make that range possible. They do not make the team equivalent to a 50-person company.

That distinction is the experiment.

When execution becomes easier to obtain, judgment, taste, organizational memory, governance, and feedback may become the real constraints. We are documenting what happens while we test that idea inside a real company—including the moments when it works, fails, or produces a result we do not yet understand.

## Five articles, five stages of learning

### [000 The Founder Was Away. The Company Kept Growing](stories/000-founder-away.md)

**What we learned:** AI can keep sensing, reading, and organizing while a founder is away. But candidate knowledge must pass human review before it becomes company memory for which the organization is willing to take responsibility.

### [001 Why Installing Copilot Still Doesn’t Make a Company AI-Native](stories/001-why-copilot-is-not-ai-native.md)

**What we learned:** Making each person’s local task faster does not make the whole company faster. AI-native requires redesigning the complete workflow of memory, permissions, handoffs, validation, and feedback.

### [002 Why Saving 30% of Your Time Still Isn’t Enough](stories/002-why-saving-30-percent-is-not-enough.md)

**What we learned:** AI’s larger value is not merely saving hours. It lets people cross role boundaries and lets a company test assumptions before every key position has been hired—without removing the need for professional judgment and responsibility.

### [003 Why Can’t Traditional Hierarchies Keep Up with AI Execution Speed?](stories/003-why-traditional-hierarchy-cannot-keep-up.md)

**What we learned:** When signals can enter shared memory directly and agents can process them continuously within boundaries, an organization can compress cross-department waits. Speed still cannot remove professional review or final accountability.

### [004 When AI Runs in Parallel 24/7, What Becomes the Company’s Real Bottleneck?](stories/004-what-is-the-real-bottleneck.md)

**What we learned:** Tokens, agent count, and lines of code are inputs. The real bottleneck is human experience that has not yet been systematized: deciding what is worth doing, what good looks like, and how one lesson becomes reusable rules, tests, and feedback.

## What this is

This repository is a public, ongoing record of the experiments behind Ingora:

- What did we try?
- What actually happened?
- What surprised or contradicted us?
- What question are we testing next?

The outcome is unknown. That uncertainty is part of the project.

## What this is not

This is not a generic AI management guide or a claim that AI can replace every specialist.

This repository stays with the observable story. It deliberately excludes diagnostic toolkits, internal prompts, proprietary workflows, and sensitive company information.

## About Ingora

Ingora is a Singapore company experimenting with what it means to build an AI-native organization. At this early stage, access to AI talent and hardware supply chains matters, so many of the stories in this repository take place inside its Shanghai subsidiary in China.

The experiment is led by **Jacky Hsu**, who spent nearly 25 years in the technology industry at Microsoft before founding Ingora in 2026.
