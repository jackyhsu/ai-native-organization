# Why This Experiment Exists

English | [中文](../zh-CN/about/why-this-experiment.md)

**By Jacky Hsu**

Companies were designed for a world in which execution was expensive, information moved slowly, and expertise had to be assembled through jobs and departments.

AI agents change part of that equation. They can read, reason, use tools, work in parallel, and continue running without waiting for a person to type every next step. But adding agents to an old workflow does not automatically create a better organization. It may simply make the old workflow generate more output—and more mistakes.

Ingora is testing a more basic question:

> If execution is becoming abundant, how should the company itself be redesigned?

The five-person founding team is attempting to cover a range of capabilities that might previously have required dozens of people. That does not mean five people already have the productivity, depth, or reliability of a 50-person company. It means AI has lowered the cost of entering more capability areas, while exposing new limits in judgment, memory, governance, and feedback.

This repository records those limits as they appear. It will include successful experiments, failed shortcuts, uncomfortable reversals, and questions that remain open.

The goal is not to prove that Ingora has found the answer. The goal is to leave a public trail of evidence while we look for it.
